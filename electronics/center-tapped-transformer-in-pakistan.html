Product details

A center-tapped transformer is a type of transformer that has a tapped secondary winding with a central connection. This type of design allows the generation of two equal output voltages that are opposite in phase by 180 0 . The center tap is usually grounded, creating a neutral point, which is advantageous for applications like rectifier circuits and power supplies. Unlike a standard transformer that provides a single output voltage, a center-tapped transformer divides the secondary voltage into two equal parts. This design is advantageous in various applications, such as supplying two 120V outputs from a 240V source in residential systems. This configuration accommodates both low and high-voltage requirements, enhancing the transformer's versatility for different electrical needs. Construction: The construction of a center-tapped transformer is similar to a conventional transformer. It has primary and secondary windings , but its secondary winding is divided into two equal halves, with a terminal at the center point. This central terminal, or center tap, is connected to a common ground, serving as a reference point for both halves of the secondary winding. This transformer features an iron core made of laminated steel sheets, which helps in reducing eddy current losses and improves efficiency. The primary and secondary windings are wound around this core, enhancing the transformer's performance and reliability. Working Principle: When an alternating current is applied to the primary winding, it generates a magnetic flux in the core. This flux links to the secondary winding, inducing an alternating magnetic flux in the secondary. The linked magnetic flux induces an alternating current in the two halves of the secondary winding, which flows into the external circuit. The voltage induced in one part of the secondary winding is equal in magnitude but opposite in phase to the voltage induced in the other part. Advantages of Center-Tapped Transformer Dual Voltage Outputs: A center-tapped transformer provides two equal voltage outputs, making it ideal for applications requiring two-phase power. Improved Efficiency in Rectification : It is beneficial in full-wave rectifiers, as it allows the use of both halves of the AC cycle, thereby enhancing the efficiency of the rectification process.
