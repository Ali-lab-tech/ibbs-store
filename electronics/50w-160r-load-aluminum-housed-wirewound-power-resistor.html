Description: Marking on Device: 50W 160Ω J Device Type: Aluminum-Housed Wirewound Power Resistor Resistance Value: 160Ω Tolerance: ±5% (indicated by "J" code) Power Rating: 50W Package/Body: Gold Anodized Aluminum Clad Housing with Mounting Tabs Terminal Type: Axial Flying Leads (radial-mount tabs for chassis screw mounting) What This Component Does: This is a high-power wirewound resistor, built by winding resistive wire around a ceramic core and encasing it in an aluminum housing that acts as both a heatsink and mechanical mounting bracket. Unlike small carbon film or metal film resistors used in low-power signal circuits, this component is specifically designed to safely dissipate significant heat while providing precise, stable resistance in higher-power circuits — commonly used wherever excess current or voltage needs to be dropped, limited, or dissipated as heat in a controlled way. Core Electrical Ratings: Parameter Rating Resistance Value 160Ω Tolerance ±5% (J-code) Power Rating 50W Max Continuous Current (calculated, @ rated power) ~0.56A Housing Material Aluminum (anodized, gold finish) Mounting Two mounting tabs with screw holes (for heatsink/chassis mounting) Terminal Style Flying lead wires (axial) Typical Operating Temp Range -55°C to +155°C (standard for this resistor class) Important Usage Notes (for support/troubleshooting reference): Heatsinking significantly improves performance: While rated at 50W in free air, this resistor's aluminum housing is designed to be bolted to a metal chassis or heatsink via its mounting tabs — doing so allows it to dissipate heat far more effectively and reliably sustain closer to its full rated power. Running it at high power without any heatsink contact will cause it to run considerably hotter and may shorten its service life. Gets very hot in normal operation: Even under moderate load, this type of resistor can reach surface temperatures well above what's safe to touch. Always allow adequate clearance from surrounding components, wiring insulation, and enclosures to prevent heat damage or fire risk. Derate for continuous duty: The 50W rating typically assumes intermittent or well-ventilated conditions; for continuous, sustained loads, it's good practice to derate the resistor (i.e., operate it at 50–70% of its rated power) to improve long-term reliability and reduce thermal stress.
