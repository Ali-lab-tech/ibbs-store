Product details

The Raspberry Pi Pico 2 is the RP2350-powered successor to the original Pico, and it keeps everything that made the first board easy to live with — the 51 × 21 mm DIP footprint, the 40-pin breadboard-friendly layout, drag-and-drop USB flashing — while roughly doubling the compute underneath. Two Arm Cortex-M33 cores or two Hazard3 RISC-V cores (selectable at boot) run at up to 150 MHz , backed by 520 KB of on-chip SRAM and 4 MB of onboard QSPI flash. That extra headroom is what you feel in practice: larger MicroPython programs, deeper sensor buffers, floating-point and DSP maths that used to be out of reach, and several PIO-driven interfaces running at the same time. Existing Pico projects and add-on boards carry straight over, so you can move a working build onto this RP2350 microcontroller board without redesigning your wiring. Key Features ⚡ Dual-core, dual-architecture RP2350A — run two Arm Cortex-M33 cores or two Hazard3 RISC-V cores at up to 150 MHz, with an on-chip PLL for variable core frequency. 🧠 520 KB SRAM + 4 MB QSPI flash — nearly double the original Pico on both counts, with a 16 KB XIP cache so code runs from flash without stalling. 🔧 26 multi-function GPIO — 2 × UART, 2 × SPI, 2 × I2C, 16 PWM channels and 3 ADC inputs fed by a 12-bit 500 ksps converter. All IO is fixed at 3.3 V. 🎛️ 12 PIO state machines — three programmable IO blocks that let you build interfaces the chip has no dedicated peripheral for, such as SD card, VGA or addressable LED strips. 🔒 Hardware security block — Arm TrustZone for Cortex-M, signed boot, 8 KB of antifuse OTP for key storage, SHA-256 acceleration, a hardware TRNG and fast glitch detectors. 🔋 Flexible 1.8–5.5 V supply — an on-board buck-boost SMPS runs the board from micro-USB, a single Li-ion cell or 3 × AA in series, so battery projects need no extra regulator. 📏 Breadboard or surface-mount — 51 × 21 mm, 1 mm thick PCB on a 2.54 mm (0.1″) grid, with castellated edges so the same board can be reflow-soldered as a module. 🛠️ Easy to program and debug — drag-and-drop UF2 over micro-USB, plus a 3-pin ARM Serial Wire Debug (SWD) header for real breakpoint debugging. ✅ C/C++ and MicroPython — a mature SDK, thousands of examples and full documentation, backed by production until at least January 2040.
