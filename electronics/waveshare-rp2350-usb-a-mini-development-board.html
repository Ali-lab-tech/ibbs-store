Product details

The Waveshare RP2350-USB-A is a 33 × 17.5 mm development board built around the Raspberry Pi RP2350A — and its headline trick is the full-size USB Type-A port on board. Driven through the RP2350's PIO block, that port lets the microcontroller act as a USB host, so a keyboard, mouse, gamepad or other USB device plugs straight into your project instead of needing a separate host controller. A second USB Type-C connector handles power and programming, with the usual drag-and-drop UF2 flashing over USB mass storage. Underneath sits the same dual-core, dual-architecture silicon as the Raspberry Pi Pico 2: two Arm Cortex-M33 cores or two Hazard3 RISC-V cores at up to 150 MHz , 520 KB of SRAM and 2 MB of onboard flash. Castellated edges mean the whole RP2350-USB-A mini development board can be reflow-soldered onto your own carrier PCB once the prototype works. Key Features 🔌 Onboard USB Type-A host port — implemented via PIO and compatible with USB 2.0/1.1 transmission, so the board can talk to standard USB peripherals directly. ⚡ Dual-core, dual-architecture RP2350A — run two Arm Cortex-M33 cores or two Hazard3 RISC-V cores, with a flexible clock up to 150 MHz. 🧠 520 KB SRAM and 2 MB onboard flash — a W25Q16JVUXIQ NOR flash holds your program, with accelerated floating-point libraries built into the chip. 🔧 15 multi-function GPIO — 2 × SPI, 2 × I2C, 2 × UART, 4 × 12-bit ADC and 14 controllable PWM channels, all remappable across the pins. 🎛️ 12 PIO state machines — the programmable IO blocks that make the USB-A port possible, and that you can reuse for custom protocols, LED strips or precise timing. 🌈 Onboard WS2812 RGB LED — a full-colour status indicator on GP16, plus dedicated BOOT and RESET buttons. 📏 Castellated mini form factor — 33.0 × 17.5 mm on a 2.54 mm pitch, light enough at roughly 8.6 g to drop into wearables, drones or tight enclosures, and solderable as an SMD module. 🔋 RT9013-33GB 500 mA LDO — low-noise, ultra-fast-response regulation, with low-power sleep and dormant modes for battery work. 🛠️ C/C++ SDK and Arduino IDE — the official Raspberry Pi Pico SDK works from the command line or from VS Code and Eclipse, backed by Waveshare's wiki and demo code.
